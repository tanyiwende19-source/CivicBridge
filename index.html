Why this matters for CALD communities

Warnings are only useful when people can understand, trust, and act on them.

01

Language barriers

Official wording can be hard to understand, especially during stress or when emergency terms have no direct translation.

Demo response: Mandarin explanation, short English, and voice playback.
02

Limited social networks

New migrants and international students may not know who to ask for help, transport, or local emergency updates.

Demo response: one-tap request goes to community support queue.
03

Limited local risk knowledge

People may not know which roads flood first, where the nearest safe place is, or when leaving becomes unsafe.

Demo response: suburb-specific risk, closures, and nearest safe place.
04

Reduced access to trusted information and services

Support can be missed when messages are spread across unfamiliar agencies, platforms, and service systems.

Demo response: official warning is turned into a guided support pathway.

English voice-ready explanation

Three action steps

  1. Pack medicine, ID, phone charger, and essential baby or family items.
  2. Stay away from flooded roads and check the map for closures.
  3. Tap “I need transport” if you cannot reach the evacuation centre safely.

What the dashboard is tracking

Understanding gaps and practical needs are separated.

Some residents understand the warning but need transport, medicine, or shelter. Others need the message explained before they can act. The dashboard keeps those signals visible instead of treating outreach as a single broadcast.

Why this helps responders

Support can be targeted by suburb, language, need type, and urgency.

Sunnybank shows Mandarin transport demand, while Runcorn shows residents who cannot evacuate. This helps community groups prioritise bilingual volunteers, rides, calls, and welfare checks.

Support pathway

Contact

2

Transport

Community support coordinates a ride to the nearest evacuation centre.

3

Settle

Family receives translated centre information, food, water, and medicine checks.

4

Follow up

Status changes from Pending to Contacted, then Resolved once safe support is confirmed.

Short-term support

  • Confirm the family has transport and knows the pickup point.
  • Provide Mandarin instructions for evacuation centre registration.
  • Check medicine, food, water, phone charging, and child or elder needs.

After the flood peak

  • Send translated return-home safety checks.
  • Connect the family with recovery payments and local support services.
  • Update the request status so community partners know the family is safe.
